\begin{keyidea}{Goals and logical structure}
A vanilla smile asks us to replace a single constant volatility by one
coherent model. This lecture follows five steps:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{vanilla prices}&\longrightarrow\text{risk-neutral terminal densities}\\
&\longrightarrow\text{a local-volatility diffusion}\\
&\longrightarrow\text{random variance and its pricing measure}\\
&\longrightarrow\text{pricing and hedging, with a barrier replicated under }\Pp\\
&\longrightarrow\text{parametrization (state, }\Q\text{, }\Pp\text{) and simulation}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Local volatility keeps one Brownian risk. Heston adds a variance factor,
so choosing pricing dynamics and identifying hedge instruments become
separate, explicit parts of the problem.
\end{keyidea}

\section{From implied volatility to a local diffusion}
\subsection{Conventions and three different volatility objects}
Throughout, the stock pays no dividends, the interest rate \(r\) is
constant, and options are European. Time is measured in years and all
prices are in the same currency. Under a selected pricing measure \(\Q\),
discounted traded prices are assumed to be true martingales. Smoothness,
integrability, and boundary assumptions will be stated where needed.

For a call quote \(C^{\rm mkt}(K,T)\), implied volatility solves
\[
C^{\rm mkt}(K,T)=C^{\rm BS}(S_0,K,T,r,\sigma_{\rm imp}(K,T)).
\]
Constant-volatility Black--Scholes would give the same implied volatility
for every strike and maturity. A smile or skew is strike dependence; its
term structure is maturity dependence. For example, a strike-80 put quoted
at 28\% and an ATM call quoted at 20\% cannot both be fitted by a single
constant volatility. Assigning each option its own BS input is a quotation
convention; it does not specify one stock process.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{L{.23\textwidth}L{.42\textwidth}L{.26\textwidth}}
\toprule Object & Meaning & How it is obtained\\\midrule
Historical volatility & A summary of past returns & Estimate from observations\\[.4em]
Implied volatility & A price expressed in BS units & Invert a vanilla quote\\[.4em]
Instantaneous variance & The squared diffusion coefficient in a model & Specify and calibrate dynamics\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Local volatility retains one Brownian risk}
\begin{definition}[Local-volatility model]
Under \(\Q\), the stock follows
\[
\boxed{\dd S_t=rS_t\dd t+\sigma_{\rm loc}(t,S_t)S_t\dd W_t^\Q.}
\]
The function \((t,s)\mapsto\sigma_{\rm loc}(t,s)\) is deterministic.
The current state is \((t,S_t)\), and the diffusion has one Brownian driver.
\end{definition}
For a fixed payoff \(g(S_T)\), let \(u(t,s)\) denote its value. It\^o gives
\[
\dd u=\left(u_t+rsu_s+\tfrac12\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(t,s)s^2u_{ss}\right)\dd t
       +\sigma_{\rm loc}(t,s)s u_s\dd W_t^\Q.
\]
A self-financing replicating portfolio with \(\Delta=u_s\) shares has gain
\(\dd u=\Delta\dd S+r(u-\Delta S)\dd t\). Equating drifts yields
\[
\boxed{u_t+rsu_s+\tfrac12\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(t,s)s^2u_{ss}-ru=0,
\qquad u(T,s)=g(s).}
\]
This replication statement requires the usual regularity and
nondegeneracy assumptions. Black--Scholes is the special case
\(\sigma_{\rm loc}(t,s)\equiv\sigma\).

\begin{warningbox}{Two variable pairs, two different equations}
For one contract, \(u(t,s;T,K)\) varies with valuation time \(t\) and current
spot \(s\); \((T,K)\) are fixed. The backward pricing PDE uses
\(u_t,u_s,u_{ss}\). In contrast,
\[
C(T,K)=u(0,S_0;T,K)
\]
is today's price surface: fix \((0,S_0)\), and vary the contract's maturity
and strike. Dupire uses \(C_T,C_K,C_{KK}\), with the other contract variable
held fixed in each partial derivative.
\end{warningbox}

\section{Deriving Dupire from prices and a density}
\subsection{Strike derivatives reveal tails and densities}
Assume \(S_T\) has a smooth density \(p(T,x)\) under \(\Q\), conditional on
\(S_0\). The integration variable \(x\) is a future stock price, and the
density does not depend on the option's strike:
\[
C(T,K)=e^{-rT}\int_K^\infty(x-K)p(T,x)\dd x.
\]
Leibniz's rule includes the moving lower boundary:
\begin{align*}
C_K(T,K)
&=e^{-rT}\left[-(K-K)p(T,K)+\int_K^\infty(-1)p(T,x)\dd x\right]\\
&=-e^{-rT}\int_K^\infty p(T,x)\dd x
=-e^{-rT}\Q(S_T>K).
\end{align*}
The lower-boundary term is zero because the payoff vanishes at \(x=K\).
Differentiating the remaining tail integral once more gives
\[
\boxed{C_{KK}(T,K)=e^{-rT}p(T,K).}
\]
Consequently, calls decrease with strike and are convex in strike, with
\(-e^{-rT}\leq C_K\leq0\) and \(C_{KK}\geq0\). A tight call spread
approximates a digital payoff; a tight butterfly measures curvature.
The density is inferred from prices under \(\Q\), rather than from
historical return frequencies. If atoms are present, the derivative
identities must be interpreted using distributional derivatives.

\subsection{Derive the forward equation using a test function}
Set \(a(t,x)=\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(t,x)x^2\), and take a smooth function
\(\varphi\) with compact support in \((0,\infty)\). It\^o and a zero-mean
stochastic integral give
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd T}\E^\Q[\varphi(S_T)]
=\E^\Q\left[rS_T\varphi'(S_T)+\tfrac12a(T,S_T)\varphi''(S_T)\right].
\]
In terms of the density,
\[
\int_0^\infty\varphi(x)p_T(T,x)\dd x
=\int_0^\infty\left[rx\varphi'(x)+\tfrac12a(T,x)\varphi''(x)\right]p(T,x)\dd x.
\]
Compact support removes the boundary terms. One integration by parts for
the drift and two for the diffusion produce
\begin{align*}
\int_0^\infty rxp\,\varphi'\dd x
&=-\int_0^\infty\varphi\,\partial_x(rxp)\dd x,\\
\int_0^\infty ap\,\varphi''\dd x
&=-\int_0^\infty\partial_x(ap)\varphi'\dd x
=\int_0^\infty\varphi\,\partial_{xx}(ap)\dd x.
\end{align*}
Since this holds for every such test function, the forward equation is
\[
\boxed{p_T=-\partial_x(rxp)+\tfrac12\partial_{xx}(ap).}
\]
It evolves probability. Discounting belongs to the pricing formula and
will enter when we differentiate \(C\).

\subsection{Differentiate in maturity and keep all boundary terms}
With \(K\) fixed, differentiating the discounted integral gives
\[
C_T=-rC+e^{-rT}(I_{\rm drift}+I_{\rm diff}),
\]
where
\[
I_{\rm drift}=-\int_K^\infty(x-K)\partial_x(rxp)\dd x,
\qquad
I_{\rm diff}=\tfrac12\int_K^\infty(x-K)\partial_{xx}(ap)\dd x.
\]
Assume differentiation under the integral is justified. In the following
calculations, also assume the displayed tail terms vanish.

\paragraph{The drift integral.}
Writing \(m(x)=rxp(T,x)\), integration by parts gives
\begin{align*}
I_{\rm drift}
&=-\big[(x-K)m(x)\big]_K^\infty+\int_K^\infty m(x)\dd x\\
&=r\int_K^\infty xp(T,x)\dd x.
\end{align*}
At the lower boundary, \(x-K=0\); at infinity, assume
\((x-K)m(x)\to0\). Splitting \(x=(x-K)+K\) gives
\begin{align*}
e^{-rT}\int_K^\infty xp(T,x)\dd x
&=C(T,K)+Ke^{-rT}\int_K^\infty p(T,x)\dd x\\
&=C(T,K)-KC_K(T,K).
\end{align*}
Thus \(e^{-rT}I_{\rm drift}=r(C-KC_K)\).

\paragraph{The diffusion integral.}
Let \(A(x)=a(T,x)p(T,x)\). Then
\begin{align*}
I_{\rm diff}
&=\tfrac12\big[(x-K)A'(x)\big]_K^\infty
  -\tfrac12\int_K^\infty A'(x)\dd x\\
&=-\tfrac12\big[A(x)\big]_K^\infty
=\tfrac12a(T,K)p(T,K).
\end{align*}
Here we assumed \((x-K)A'(x)\to0\) and \(A(x)\to0\) at infinity.
The factor \(x-K\) removes the first lower-boundary term, but the second
integration leaves \(A(K)\). This boundary at the payoff's kink explains
why the local coefficient is evaluated at spot \(K\).

\paragraph{Assemble the terms.}
Using \(e^{-rT}p(T,K)=C_{KK}(T,K)\),
\begin{align*}
C_T
&=-rC+r(C-KC_K)+\tfrac12\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(T,K)K^2C_{KK}\\
&=-rKC_K+\tfrac12\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(T,K)K^2C_{KK}.
\end{align*}
The terms \(-rC\) and \(+rC\) cancel. Therefore, wherever \(K>0\) and
\(C_{KK}>0\),
\begin{keyidea}{Dupire's local variance}
\[
\boxed{\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(T,K)
=\frac{C_T(T,K)+rKC_K(T,K)}{\tfrac12K^2C_{KK}(T,K)}.}
\]
The result is a model coefficient at time \(T\), spot \(K\). It is not
in general the implied volatility of the option with strike \(K\) and
maturity \(T\). The formula assumes no dividends and a constant rate.
\end{keyidea}
The initial surface is \(C(0,K)=(S_0-K)^+\). Under the martingale and
regularity assumptions, the boundary values are \(C(T,0)=S_0\) and
\(C(T,K)\to0\) as \(K\to\infty\). Convexity and the nonnegative numerator
\(C_T+rKC_K\) are necessary consistency checks for this diffusion.



\paragraph{Differentiation amplifies quote noise}
For equally spaced strikes,
\[
\widehat C_{KK}=\frac{C(T,K+h)-2C(T,K)+C(T,K-h)}{h^2}.
\]
If the three quote errors are independent with standard deviation
\(\varepsilon\), the error variance of this estimate is
\(6\varepsilon^2/h^4\), and its standard deviation is
\(\sqrt6\,\varepsilon/h^2\). Smaller spacing reduces truncation error for a
smooth surface but amplifies quote noise. Very small \(C_{KK}\) also makes
the Dupire ratio unstable. Fit a smooth, arbitrage-consistent surface and
check interpolation, extrapolation, and sensitivity to the grid.

\begin{keyidea}{Terminal marginals do not specify the full path law}
A sufficiently rich vanilla surface determines the risk-neutral marginal
law of \(S_T\) at each maturity. Averages, maxima, and barrier events also
need joint dynamics between dates. Local volatility supplies one diffusion
consistent with a suitable surface; another model can share the same
marginals and give different exotic prices and hedges.
\end{keyidea}

\section{Heston: a random variance factor and a pricing measure}
\subsection{Risk-neutral dynamics and parameter meanings}
Under the chosen pricing measure,
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\frac{\dd S_t}{S_t}&=r\dd t+\sqrt{V_t}\dd W_t^S,\\
\dd V_t&=\kappa(\theta-V_t)\dd t+\eta\sqrt{V_t}\dd W_t^V,
\qquad \dd\langle W^S,W^V\rangle_t=\rho\dd t.
\end{aligned}}
\]
The parameters satisfy \(V_0\geq0\), \(\kappa,\theta,\eta>0\), and
\(|\rho|\leq1\). Below, arguments requiring two independent risks assume
\(v>0\) and \(|\rho|<1\).
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cL{.72\textwidth}}
\toprule Parameter & Interpretation\\\midrule
\(V_0\) & Current instantaneous variance; instantaneous volatility is \(\sqrt{V_0}\)\\
\(\theta\) & Long-run variance level under the selected measure\\
\(\kappa\) & Speed of mean reversion of variance\\
\(\eta\) & Diffusion scale of variance, often called vol-of-vol\\
\(\rho\) & Correlation of stock and variance Brownian shocks\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The Markov state is \((S_t,V_t)\). Two states with the same spot and
different variance can have different option values.

Write \(\dd W^V=\rho\dd W^S+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\dd W^\perp\), where
\(W^S,W^\perp\) are independent. If \(\rho<0\), a negative stock shock
contributes positively to the variance shock. This mechanism can produce
a downside equity skew, although independent variance shocks and parameter
interactions remain.

\subsection{Conditional means and the CIR boundary}
Taking expectations in the variance SDE gives
\[
m'(u)=\kappa(\theta-m(u)),\qquad m(t)=v,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\E^\Q[V_T\mid V_t=v]=\theta+(v-\theta)e^{-\kappa(T-t)}.}
\]
For \(v=0.09\), \(\theta=0.04\), \(\kappa=2\), and \(T-t=0.5\), the mean is
\(0.04+0.05e^{-1}\simeq0.0584\). Its square root is about 24.2\%.
By concavity, \(\E[\sqrt{V_T}\mid V_t]\leq\sqrt{\E[V_T\mid V_t]}\);
these are different objects.

The CIR variance process remains nonnegative. Starting from \(V_0>0\),
the Feller condition \(2\kappa\theta\geq\eta^2\) makes zero unattainable;
when it fails, the process may reach zero. This condition concerns the
continuous process, not a discretization's ability to remain nonnegative.

\subsection{The implied-volatility surface moves with the variance}
In Heston, every vanilla price is a function \(C(t,S_t,V_t;K,T)\). Inverting
the BS formula maturity by maturity and strike by strike, every implied
volatility is therefore a function \(\Sigma(t,S_t,V_t;K,T)\). When \(V_t\)
moves, the whole surface moves, including on days when the stock is unchanged.

The average expected variance provides a deterministic-variance approximation
to the ATM implied variance. When \(\eta=0\), variance follows its mean ODE,
and BS with the time-averaged variance is exact at every strike:
\[
\Sigma_{\rm ATM}^2(t,T)\simeq\frac1{T-t}\int_t^T\E^\Q[V_u\mid V_t]\dd u
=\theta+(V_t-\theta)\,\frac{1-e^{-\kappa(T-t)}}{\kappa(T-t)}.
\]
With \(\kappa=2\), \(\theta=0.04\), a move of \(V_t\) from 0.04 to 0.09
raises the three-month ATM level from 20\% to about 28.2\%, and the two-year
level only to about 22.9\%, within this approximation. The short end moves
most. Mean reversion anchors average expected variance at \(\theta\);
for \(\eta>0\), option-implied levels also depend on the distribution of
variance and its correlation with stock shocks.

\begin{keyidea}{Surface risk distinguishes the two models}
In a local-volatility model, the future surface is a deterministic function
of \((t,S_t)\): it moves only through the stock. In Heston, the variance has
its own shock \(Z_2\) under \(\Pp\), so the surface carries a separate risk.
The stock cannot hedge it. The next subsection explains how it is priced;
Section~\ref{sec:barrier} shows how an option hedges it.
\end{keyidea}

\subsection{From historical dynamics to a pricing measure}\label{sec:measures}
Under \(\Pp\), use independent Brownian coordinates
\(Z_1^\Pp,Z_2^\Pp\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\dd S_t}{S_t}&=\mu\dd t+\sqrt{V_t}\dd Z_{1,t}^\Pp,\\
\dd V_t&=\kappa_\Pp(\theta_\Pp-V_t)\dd t
+\eta\sqrt{V_t}\bigl(\rho\dd Z_{1,t}^\Pp+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\dd Z_{2,t}^\Pp\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Suppose a valid equivalent measure change is defined by
\[
\dd Z_{j,t}^\Q=\dd Z_{j,t}^\Pp+\lambda_{j,t}\dd t,\qquad j=1,2.
\]
In particular, the stochastic exponential defining the density must be
a true martingale; the algebra below alone does not establish this.
On \(V_t>0\), the stock drift becomes \(\mu-\sqrt{V_t}\lambda_{1,t}\).
Requiring it to equal \(r\) fixes
\[
\boxed{\lambda_{1,t}=\frac{\mu-r}{\sqrt{V_t}}.}
\]
The corresponding variance drift is
\[
b_\Q(t,s,v)=b_\Pp(v)-\pi(t,s,v),\qquad
\pi_t=\eta\sqrt{V_t}
\bigl(\rho\lambda_{1,t}+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\lambda_{2,t}\bigr).
\]
The stock restriction leaves \(\lambda_2\) free. With stock and cash only,
no-arbitrage does not select a unique drift for variance. Option prices
provide information about this remaining risk premium. Equivalent changes
of measure change drifts, while preserving quadratic variations and
covariations, hence \(\eta\) and \(\rho\) within this model specification.

\begin{boardexample}{an affine variance drift adjustment}
Choose the convention \(\pi(v)=\lambda v\). Then
\[
b_\Q(v)=\kappa_\Pp\theta_\Pp-(\kappa_\Pp+\lambda)v
=\kappa_\Q(\theta_\Q-v),
\]
with
\[
\boxed{\kappa_\Q=\kappa_\Pp+\lambda,\qquad
\theta_\Q=\frac{\kappa_\Pp\theta_\Pp}{\kappa_\Pp+\lambda},}
\qquad\kappa_\Pp+\lambda>0.
\]
As in the barrier case study, \(\kappa_\Pp=1.6\), \(\theta_\Pp=0.05\), and
\(\lambda=0.4\) give \(\kappa_\Q=2\), \(\theta_\Q=0.04\), with
\(\kappa_\Q\theta_\Q=\kappa_\Pp\theta_\Pp=0.08\). The sign convention is
\(b_\Q=b_\Pp-\lambda v\); \(\lambda\) need not be positive. Admissibility
of the measure change remains an assumption.
\end{boardexample}
From now on, \(\kappa,\theta\) denote risk-neutral parameters.

\section{Pricing and hedging in Heston}
\subsection{It\^o's formula and the mixed derivative}
For \(u(t,S_t,V_t)\), the quadratic variation terms are
\[
(\dd S_t)^2=V_tS_t^2\dd t,\qquad
(\dd V_t)^2=\eta^2V_t\dd t,\qquad
\dd S_t\dd V_t=\rho\eta S_tV_t\dd t.
\]
The drift in It\^o's formula is \(u_t+\mathcal L^\Q u\), where
\[
\mathcal L^\Q u=rsu_s+\kappa(\theta-v)u_v
+\tfrac12vs^2u_{ss}+\rho\eta svu_{sv}+\tfrac12\eta^2vu_{vv}.
\]
There is no factor \(1/2\) in front of the mixed term: the symmetric
terms in the Hessian combine. For payoff \(h(S_T)\), the pricing
representation is
\[
u(t,s,v)=e^{-r(T-t)}\E^\Q[h(S_T)\mid S_t=s,V_t=v].
\]
Subject to sufficient regularity and appropriate boundary/growth
conditions, the discounted value is a martingale and
\begin{keyidea}{The Heston pricing PDE}
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
0={}&u_t+rsu_s+\kappa(\theta-v)u_v+\tfrac12vs^2u_{ss}\\
&+\rho\eta svu_{sv}+\tfrac12\eta^2vu_{vv}-ru,
\qquad u(T,s,v)=h(s).
\end{aligned}}
\]
For a call, \(h(s)=(s-K)^+\). The variance drift under \(\Q\) has been
specified and calibrated; stock-and-cash no-arbitrage alone did not fix it.
\end{keyidea}

\subsection{Why stock delta alone is insufficient}
The random part of the claim's change is
\[
s\sqrt v\,u_s\dd W^S+\eta\sqrt v\,u_v\dd W^V.
\]
Subtracting \(\Delta=u_s\) shares leaves
\[
\eta\sqrt v\,u_v\bigl(\rho\dd W^S+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\dd W^\perp\bigr).
\]
Changing the stock holding can offset the correlated component. In fact,
\(\Delta=u_s+(\rho\eta/s)u_v\) cancels the common \(W^S\) exposure
instantaneously. It still leaves
\(\eta\sqrt v\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,u_v\dd W^\perp\). When this coefficient
is nonzero, no stock position can eliminate it. This is the local source
of incompleteness with stock and cash alone.

\subsection{A second traded option spans the variance exposure}
Let \(g(t,s,v)\) be a traded hedge option priced consistently with the
same dynamics. Hold \(\Delta\) shares, \(\alpha\) hedge options, and
an amount \(b\) in the bank account. The portfolio value is
\(u=\Delta s+\alpha g+b\), and its self-financing gain is
\[
\dd u=\Delta\dd S+\alpha\dd g+rb\dd t.
\]
The remaining random terms after hedging are
\[
\sqrt v\left[s(u_s-\Delta-\alpha g_s)\dd W^S
+\eta(u_v-\alpha g_v)\dd W^V\right].
\]
For \(v>0\), \(\eta>0\), and \(|\rho|<1\), cancellation requires both
coefficients to vanish. If \(g_v\ne0\),
\[
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{u_v}{g_v},\qquad
\Delta=u_s-\frac{u_v}{g_v}g_s,\qquad
b=u-\Delta s-\alpha g.}
\]
First match variance exposure with the option, then match stock exposure
with shares. If both option values satisfy the same pricing PDE, their
drifts also match the self-financing portfolio drift. A global replication
argument additionally requires the hedge option to remain available with
nonzero variance sensitivity, adequate regularity, and an admissible
trading strategy over the hedge horizon.

\begin{warningbox}{Variance sensitivity and practical hedge limits}
The derivatives \(u_v,g_v\) are with respect to the model's state variance.
They are different from BS vega with respect to an implied volatility.
A small \(g_v\) can produce an unstable, very large position. Discrete
rebalancing, transaction costs, illiquidity, and model misspecification
leave practical hedge error even when the formal diffusion risks are spanned.
\end{warningbox}

\section{Case study: replicating a barrier option under the historical measure}\label{sec:barrier}
This section carries out the replication argument of the previous section
step by step, along the paths the market actually follows. The claim is
path-dependent, and the hedge uses a liquid call.

\subsection{Contract, instruments, and assumptions}
A bank sells a \textbf{down-and-out call}: at \(T=1\) it pays
\((S_T-K)^+\) if \(\min_{0\leq t\leq T}S_t>H\), and nothing otherwise.
Monitoring is continuous and there is no rebate. Take \(K=100\), \(H=85\),
\(S_0=100\), \(V_0=0.04\), and \(r=0\).

The hedge instruments are the stock, cash, and a liquid ATM call \(g\) with
\(K_g=100\) and \(T_g=1.5\). The call outlives the claim, avoiding expiry
of the hedge instrument during the hedge horizon. We additionally assume
\(g_v\ne0\) on the states visited before exit. A later expiry alone does
not guarantee a uniform lower bound on \(g_v\); a small value still makes
\(\alpha=u_v/g_v\) unstable.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule Measure & Drift of \(S\) & \(\kappa\) & \(\theta\) & \(\eta\) & \(\rho\)\\\midrule
\(\Pp\) & \(\mu\) & 1.6 & 0.05 & 0.4 & \(-0.6\)\\
\(\Q\) & \(r=0\) & 2.0 & 0.04 & 0.4 & \(-0.6\)\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The two rows are linked by the affine premium of Section~\ref{sec:measures} with \(\lambda=0.4\):
\(\kappa_\Q=1.6+0.4=2\) and \(\theta_\Q=1.6\times0.05/2=0.04\). The
\(\Q\)-row is the baseline of the smile figures.

\begin{warningbox}{Assumptions}
Under \(\Pp\), \((S,V)\) follows Heston with the true \(\eta\) and \(\rho\).
The call trades at its Heston \(\Q\)-price, i.e.\ the model is calibrated to
the call market. Trading is continuous and costless, and \(V_t\) is known.
\end{warningbox}

\subsection{Step 1: It\^o along the \texorpdfstring{\(\Pp\)}{P}-paths}
Write \(W^S=Z_1^\Pp\) and \(W^V=\rho Z_1^\Pp+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,Z_2^\Pp\).
For a smooth price \(f(t,S_t,V_t)\), meaning the claim \(u\) while it is alive
or the call \(g\),
\[
\dd f=\mathcal A^\Pp f\dd t+\sqrt{V_t}\,S_tf_s\dd W_t^S+\eta\sqrt{V_t}\,f_v\dd W_t^V,
\]
\[
\mathcal A^\Pp f=f_t+\mu sf_s+\kappa_\Pp(\theta_\Pp-v)f_v
+\tfrac12vs^2f_{ss}+\rho\eta svf_{sv}+\tfrac12\eta^2vf_{vv}.
\]
Hold \(\Delta_t\) shares, \(\alpha_t\) calls, and cash \(b_t\). The portfolio
\(X_t=\Delta_tS_t+\alpha_tg_t+b_t\) is self-financing when
\[
\dd X_t=\Delta_t\dd S_t+\alpha_t\dd g_t+rb_t\dd t.
\]
These are pathwise identities. No expectation, and no probability of any
event, has been used.

\subsection{Step 2: cancel both Brownian exposures}
Subtracting,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dd(X_t-u_t)={}&\bigl[\Delta\mu s+\alpha\mathcal A^\Pp g+rb-\mathcal A^\Pp u\bigr]\dd t\\
&+\sqrt v\,s\,(\Delta+\alpha g_s-u_s)\dd W^S+\eta\sqrt v\,(\alpha g_v-u_v)\dd W^V.
\end{aligned}
\]
Write \(\tau=\inf\{t:S_t\le H\}\). Start from the sale proceeds, \(X_0=u_0\),
and seek a self-financing portfolio satisfying \(X_t=u(t,S_t,V_t)\) for
\(t<\tau\wedge T\). Matching first the independent \(Z_2^\Pp\) exposure,
then \(Z_1^\Pp\), and finally the portfolio value gives
\[
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{u_v}{g_v},\qquad\Delta=u_s-\alpha g_s,\qquad b=u-\Delta s-\alpha g.}
\]
The holdings leave no Brownian exposure in ``portfolio minus claim''.
Matching the drifts supplies a condition on \(u\): once the holdings have
been fixed, no further degree of freedom remains. The pricing equation below
makes this strategy self-financing with \(X=u\). At \(\tau<T\), both values
are zero and the hedge is liquidated; on survival to \(T\), the portfolio
delivers \((S_T-K)^+\).

\subsection{Step 3: a single market price of variance risk}
Match \(\mathcal A^\Pp u=\Delta\mu s+\alpha\mathcal A^\Pp g+rb\),
then substitute \(\Delta\) and \(b\). The terms in \(\mu\) combine:
\[
\begin{aligned}
0&=\mathcal A^\Pp u-\alpha\mathcal A^\Pp g-\mu s(u_s-\alpha g_s)
 -r\bigl(u-(u_s-\alpha g_s)s-\alpha g\bigr)\\
&=\bigl[\mathcal A^\Pp u-ru-(\mu-r)su_s\bigr]
 -\alpha\bigl[\mathcal A^\Pp g-rg-(\mu-r)sg_s\bigr]=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(\alpha=u_v/g_v\), at points where \(u_v\ne0\), dividing gives
\[
\boxed{\frac{\mathcal A^\Pp u-ru-(\mu-r)su_s}{u_v}
=\frac{\mathcal A^\Pp g-rg-(\mu-r)sg_s}{g_v}=:\pi(t,s,v).}
\]
The left side involves only the claim, the right side only the call. Pairing
any two traded claims with the same call shows that the ratio is one function
of the state, shared by all traded claims. It is the market price of variance
risk per unit of exposure \(u_v\), and it coincides with the drift adjustment
\(\pi\) of Section~\ref{sec:measures}. Where \(u_v=0\), use the undivided
identity; the pricing PDE still applies. The formula in terms of \(g\)
requires the whole price function and its derivatives, as well as the
historical dynamics. One observed call price supplies none of these by itself.

\subsection{What call prices identify: the pricing drift}
In the case study, the liquid call trades at its calibrated Heston price,
so \(g\) solves
\[
g_t+rsg_s+\kappa_\Q(\theta_\Q-v)g_v+
\tfrac12vs^2g_{ss}+\rho\eta svg_{sv}+\tfrac12\eta^2vg_{vv}-rg=0.
\]
Substitution into the ratio above cancels the stock drift and all
second derivatives, leaving
\[
\boxed{\pi(v)=b_\Pp(v)-b_\Q(v)
=\kappa_\Pp(\theta_\Pp-v)-\kappa_\Q(\theta_\Q-v)=0.4v.}
\]
Calibration uses several strikes and maturities to select the \(\Q\)-parameters.
Together with the model assumption, these parameters specify a price
function \(g(t,s,v)\) in each state. Splitting the fitted drift into
\(b_\Pp\) and \(\pi\) additionally requires estimates of
\(\kappa_\Pp,\theta_\Pp\) from the variance history. The stock drift \(\mu\)
is absent from this difference of variance drifts.

\begin{keyidea}{One option for hedging; a surface for calibration}
A liquid call with \(g_v\ne0\), together with stock and cash, spans both
Brownian risks within the specified model. A single price observation
does not identify that model. Prices and hedge ratios need the calibrated
\(b_\Q\), without estimating the historical drift and its risk premium
separately.
\end{keyidea}

\subsection{Step 4: the \texorpdfstring{\(\Q\)}{Q}-PDE with an absorbing boundary}
Rearranging the identity for \(u\),
\[
u_t+rsu_s+\bigl[\kappa_\Pp(\theta_\Pp-v)-\pi\bigr]u_v
+\tfrac12vs^2u_{ss}+\rho\eta svu_{sv}+\tfrac12\eta^2vu_{vv}-ru=0.
\]
With \(\pi=\lambda v\), the bracket is \(\kappa_\Q(\theta_\Q-v)\): this is the
Heston PDE under \(\Q\). The contract supplies the domain and the boundary
conditions:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{claim: }&s>H,\ t<T,\qquad u(t,H,v)=0,\qquad u(T,s,v)=(s-K)^+;\\
\text{call: }&s>0,\ t<T_g,\qquad g(T_g,s,v)=(s-K_g)^+.
\end{aligned}
\]
\begin{keyidea}{What happened to the historical drifts}
\(\mu\) has disappeared, and \(\kappa_\Pp,\theta_\Pp\) enter only through the
combination \(\kappa_\Pp(\theta_\Pp-v)-\pi=\kappa_\Q(\theta_\Q-v)\). The hedge is
built and verified along \(\Pp\)-paths; prices and hedge ratios come from \(\Q\).
\end{keyidea}


\section{Parametrizing the model: state, \texorpdfstring{\(\Q\)}{Q}, and \texorpdfstring{\(\Pp\)}{P}}\label{sec:param}
\subsection{Three kinds of inputs}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{L{.1\textwidth}L{.2\textwidth}L{.2\textwidth}L{.38\textwidth}}
\toprule Input & Role & Same under \(\Pp\) and \(\Q\)? & Where it comes from\\\midrule
\(S_t,V_t\) & state & yes: same paths & \(S_t\) observed; \(V_t\) is not observed and is refitted daily from options\\[.3em]
\(\eta,\rho\) & diffusion coefficients & yes & options; cross-check on time series\\[.3em]
\(\kappa,\theta\) & variance drift & no & \(\Q\): option prices; \(\Pp\): time series\\[.3em]
\(\mu\) & stock drift & \(\Pp\) only & not needed to price or to hedge\\[.3em]
\(r\) & discounting & --- & rates market\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Prices and hedge ratios use \(\Q\)-parameters. Scenario probabilities, risk
limits, and the expected P\&L of an imperfect hedge use \(\Pp\). The
delta-only drift \(-\pi u_v\) of Section~\ref{sec:barrier} is an example of
the last kind.

\subsection{Why \texorpdfstring{\(\eta\)}{eta} and \texorpdfstring{\(\rho\)}{rho} cannot change with the measure}
Quadratic variation and covariation are computed path by path:
\[
\langle V\rangle_t=\int_0^t\eta^2V_u\dd u,\qquad
\langle\log S,V\rangle_t=\int_0^t\rho\eta V_u\dd u.
\]
Each is the limit in probability of sums of squared (or cross) increments
along refining partitions, and a subsequence converges almost surely.
Equivalent measures share the same null sets, so these limits are the same
under \(\Pp\) and \(\Q\). Girsanov changes drifts only.

\begin{studentexercise}{a calibrated and an estimated correlation}
Options calibrate \(\rho=-0.6\); a time-series estimate gives \(\rho=-0.3\).
Is this a legitimate \(\Pp\)-versus-\(\Q\) difference?
\end{studentexercise}
\begin{shortsolution}
Not within Heston. Either the estimate is noisy, since \(V\) is not
observed and its proxies (realized variance, variance-index levels) add
error, or the model is misspecified. The barrier hedge relied on the true
\(\rho\) being the one used in the PDE.
\end{shortsolution}

\subsection{Calibration to vanilla prices}
For observed vanilla prices \(C_i^{\rm mkt}\), a price-based calibration is
\[
\min_{\vartheta}\sum_iw_i
\left(C^{\rm Heston}(K_i,T_i;\vartheta)-C_i^{\rm mkt}\right)^2,
\qquad \vartheta=(V_0,\kappa,\theta,\eta,\rho).
\]
Choose the instrument set, positive weights, bid--ask treatment, and
parameter constraints explicitly. Price-error and implied-volatility-error
objectives assign different effective weights. Use several starting points;
inspect parameter stability and the full smile across maturities, not only
a single aggregate error. One ATM quote cannot generally identify all
five parameters, and a good fit need not make every parameter well identified.

These are pricing parameters under \(\Q\). Estimating a time series under
\(\Pp\) addresses a different problem. A richer model does not automatically
fit every arbitrage-free surface, and fitting vanillas does not validate
its joint path dynamics for every exotic payoff.

\begin{tabular}{cL{.74\textwidth}}
\toprule Parameter & Effect to examine in the controlled comparisons\\\midrule
\(\rho\) & More negative correlation strengthens downside skew in the experiment.\\[.4em]
\(\eta\) & Changes wings, curvature, and ATM level together; an increase does not simply shift the whole smile upward.\\[.4em]
\(V_0\) & Has a larger influence on the short-maturity smile in the experiment.\\[.4em]
\(\theta\) & Has a larger influence at the longer maturity in the experiment.\\[.4em]

\end{tabular}
\end{center}



\subsection{Expected average variance and the term structure}\label{sec:term}
Integrating the conditional-mean formula from time zero gives
\[
\boxed{w(T):=\frac1T\E^\Q\!\left[\int_0^T V_t\dd t\right]
=\theta+(V_0-\theta)A(\kappa T),\qquad
A(x)=\frac{1-e^{-x}}x.}
\]
The identity is exact for Heston and, at deterministic rates, \(w(T)\)
is the fair strike of a variance swap paying
\(T^{-1}\int_0^T V_t\dd t-K_{\rm var}\) at \(T\). The weight satisfies
\(A(x)=1-x/2+O(x^2)\) near zero and \(A(x)\sim1/x\) for large \(x\).
Consequently \(w(T)\to V_0\) as \(T\to0\), and \(w(T)\to\theta\) as
\(T\to\infty\). The weight is one half at \(\kappa T\simeq1.594\).
Larger \(\kappa\) removes the influence of \(V_0-\theta\) more quickly.
When \(V_0<\theta\), \(w\) increases with maturity; when \(V_0>\theta\),
it decreases.

\begin{warningbox}{Expected variance and ATM implied variance}
\(\Sigma_{\rm ATM}^2(T)\approx w(T)\) is a deterministic-variance approximation,
exact when \(\eta=0\). With stochastic variance, neither equality nor the
long-maturity limit \(\Sigma_{\rm ATM}^2\to\theta\) follows from the mean
variance formula. Use the Heston pricer for calibration and for the
controlled comparisons in the Lecture 11 slides.
\end{warningbox}


\subsection{ATM skew: a guide for sign, size, and maturity}\label{sec:skew}
Let \(F_{0,T}=S_0e^{rT}\), \(k=\log(K/F_{0,T})\), and \(\Sigma(k,T)\)
denote BS implied volatility. For the flat-mean baseline \(V_0=\theta\),
the leading small-\(\eta\) expansion gives
\[
\boxed{\partial_k\Sigma^2(0,T)\approx
\frac{\rho\eta}{\kappa T}
\left[1-\frac{1-e^{-\kappa T}}{\kappa T}\right].}
\]
See R. Lee, \href{https://math.uchicago.edu/~rogerlee/impvol.pdf}
{\emph{Implied Volatility: Statics, Dynamics, and Probabilistic Interpretation}},
Example 3.9. The restriction \(V_0=\theta\) matters for this simple finite-maturity
formula; it is not a formula for arbitrary forward variance curves.
Since \(1-A(\kappa T)\sim\kappa T/2\) near zero and
\(\partial_k\Sigma^2=2\Sigma\partial_k\Sigma\), the short-time guide is
\[
\partial_k\Sigma(0,T)\longrightarrow\frac{\rho\eta}{4\sqrt{V_0}}.
\]
For large \(\kappa T\), the finite-maturity guide instead behaves like
\(\rho\eta/(\kappa T)\) for the variance skew. Thus the sign follows
\(\rho\), the size scales with \(\eta\), and mean reversion damps the
longer-dated skew within this approximation. Curvature and ATM shifts
require further terms or the full pricer.


\subsection{A calibration protocol}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Clean the quotes.} Use mid prices inside the bid--ask spread.
Remove stale quotes and quotes that violate static no-arbitrage (calendar
spreads, butterflies).
\item \textbf{Choose the error.} Price errors overweight expensive options.
A first-order expansion of the BS price in its volatility gives
\[
C^{\rm model}-C^{\rm mkt}\simeq\mathcal V^{\rm BS}\bigl(\Sigma^{\rm model}-\Sigma^{\rm mkt}\bigr),
\]
so dividing price errors by BS vega approximately measures errors in implied
volatility without inverting the model price.
\item \textbf{Impose constraints.} Require \(V_0,\kappa,\theta,\eta>0\) and
\(|\rho|<1\), and report the Feller ratio \(2\kappa\theta/\eta^2\).
\item \textbf{Start from the parameter guides} above, then from several
other starting points, and compare the minima.
\item \textbf{Validate.} Compare residuals with bid--ask widths, test
out-of-sample strikes and maturities, and check stability from one day to the next.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{L{.34\textwidth}L{.08\textwidth}L{.46\textwidth}}
\toprule Feature of the implied surface & Lever & Reason\\\midrule
Short-dated ATM level & \(V_0\) & \(\Sigma_{\rm ATM}^2\to V_0\) as \(T\to0\)\\[.3em]
Long-dated level & \(\theta\) & anchors expected average variance\\[.3em]
Term-structure transition & \(\kappa\) & weight \((1-e^{-\kappa T})/(\kappa T)\) on \(V_0-\theta\)\\[.3em]
Skew (slope across strikes) & \(\rho\) & falls in \(S\) come with rises in \(V\)\\[.3em]
Curvature and wings & \(\eta\) & random variance fattens both tails\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are parameter guides, not identities. \(\eta\) also scales the skew
when \(\rho\neq0\), and \(\kappa\) damps both skew and curvature at long maturities.




\section{From the continuous model to a simulation}\label{sec:sim}
\subsection{Generate correlated Brownian increments}
At each time step draw independent \(Z_1,Z_2\sim N(0,1)\), then set
\[
\Delta W^S=\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z_1,\qquad
\Delta W^V=\sqrt{\Delta t}
\bigl(\rho Z_1+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}Z_2\bigr).
\]
Directly,
\[
\Var(\Delta W^S)=\Var(\Delta W^V)=\Delta t,
\qquad \Cov(\Delta W^S,\Delta W^V)=\rho\Delta t.
\]
Use a fresh independent pair at each step and for each independent path.
Using independent stock and variance increments would simulate \(\rho=0\),
rather than the requested correlated model.

\subsection{Euler can cross a boundary the exact process respects}
The naive Euler step is
\[
V_{n+1}=V_n+\kappa(\theta-V_n)\Delta t
+\eta\sqrt{V_n}\,\Delta W_n^V.
\]
A sufficiently negative normal increment produces \(V_{n+1}<0\), so the
next square root is undefined. The continuous Feller condition does not
prevent this numerical failure.

A full-truncation variance update with a log stock step uses
\(V_n^+=\max(V_n,0)\):
\begin{keyidea}{State the approximation precisely}
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
V_{n+1}&=V_n+\kappa(\theta-V_n^+)\Delta t
+\eta\sqrt{V_n^+}\,\Delta W_n^V,\\
S_{n+1}&=S_n\exp\left[(r-\tfrac12V_n^+)\Delta t
+\sqrt{V_n^+}\,\Delta W_n^S\right].
\end{aligned}}
\]
Keep the raw \(V_n\) in the first term. Truncation is applied inside the
drift and diffusion coefficients; the raw next variance can still be negative.
Use the same old-time \(V_n^+\) in the stock update, which keeps \(S_n>0\).
\end{keyidea}
This scheme approximates Heston; it is not an exact simulation. Replacing
the entire variance update by its positive part defines a different scheme.

\subsection{Check time bias separately from sampling error}
For independent paths and terminal payoff \(H^{(m)}\), estimate
\[
\widehat V_0=e^{-rT}\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^M H^{(m)},
\qquad
\widehat{\rm SE}=e^{-rT}\frac{s_H}{\sqrt M},
\]
where \(s_H\) is the sample standard deviation of the payoffs. This standard
error measures sampling variability for the simulated discretization.
It does not measure time-discretization or monitoring bias. Refine
\(\Delta t\) as well as increasing \(M\), check a vanilla against an
independent Heston price, and verify the \(\eta\to0\) limit. When
\(\eta=0\), variance follows a deterministic mean-reversion ODE;
constant-volatility BS is recovered when in addition \(V_0=\theta\).
